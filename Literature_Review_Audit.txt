MULTIVIEW THESIS - LITERATURE REVIEW AUDIT
============================================


1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
--------------------

MULTIVIEW is the software artifact behind the thesis "Unified Multimodal
Architecture for Enhanced E-Commerce Product Discovery." It's a working
full-stack prototype:

- Frontend: Next.js 15 / React 19 / Tailwind - unified search bar (text,
  image upload, voice), search results, AR viewer (@google/model-viewer,
  WebXR), checkout/payment flow, and an evaluation dashboard.
- Backend: FastAPI + PyTorch - CLIP embeddings for text/image search, FAISS
  for vector similarity retrieval, OpenAI Whisper for voice-to-text, LLaVA
  for richer visual reasoning, Pollinations.ai for generative fallback
  images when no catalog match exists.
- Data: Pix3D dataset - 10,069 furniture products, each with a real photo
  and a paired 3D model (converted to GLB for AR/3D viewing).
- Evaluation: logs CLIP relevance scores, response time, and a 7-question
  Likert survey.

Core research claim: no prior published system unifies text + image +
voice search, CLIP/LLaVA-based retrieval, generative fallback imagery,
AR/3D visualization, and checkout into one cohesive discovery pipeline -
MULTIVIEW is that unification, evaluated via Design Science Research
methodology (Hevner et al.).

The thesis's own Chapter 2 already reviews 25 sources (2023-2026). I had
agents independently re-read all 23 corresponding PDFs in the literatures
folder and re-judge relevance from scratch, rather than trusting the
thesis's own framing.

--- Highly relevant - core supporting citations ---

* Beyond Text: Text-Image Fusion for Scalable Two-Tower Retrieval
  (Zhang et al., Target, 2026)
  Two-tower CLIP text/image fusion for e-commerce search at Target;
  mixture-of-modality-experts, 3-stage curriculum training, +4.86%
  nDCG@1 over text-only, sub-100ms CPU serving via ANN indexing.
  Include? YES

* VL-CLIP (Giahi et al., Walmart, RecSys '25)
  Grounding-DINO crops products before CLIP encoding + LLM-cleaned text
  descriptions; production deployment at Walmart scale: +18.6% CTR,
  +15.5% add-to-cart, +4.0% GMV.
  Include? YES

* MOON (Zhang et al., Alibaba/Taobao, WSDM '26)
  First generative-MLLM (not dual-encoder) product representation model;
  core-product cropping, hard-negative sampling on real purchase
  triplets; SOTA vs. CLIP/SigLIP2/FashionCLIP.
  Include? YES

* MOON2.0 (Nie et al., 2025)
  Successor fixing modality imbalance via modality-driven MoE +
  dual-level alignment; releases 6.4M-sample MBE2.0 benchmark.
  Include? YES

* MOON Embedding (Fu et al., Taobao production report)
  Full 3-stage production deployment of MOON at Taobao search-ads
  scale; +20% CTR over 3 years/5 iterations.
  Include? YES

* Multimodal semantic retrieval for product search
  (Liu & Lopez Ramos, Amazon, WWW '25)
  4-tower/3-tower CLIP+BiBERT fusion vs. text-only baseline on 3.38M
  products; FAISS-based retrieval; shows CLIP alone has high relevance
  but low recall unless aligned to product data.
  Include? YES

* Turning Adversaries into Allies (Jenq & Shen, CIKM '25)
  Renders product title/metadata onto the image itself before CLIP
  encoding ("reversed typographic attack") - consistent Acc@1/Acc@3
  gains across CLIP/SigLIP/DINO on real e-commerce datasets.
  Include? YES

--- Moderately relevant - good supporting/contextual citations ---

* Visual Product Search Benchmark (Govindappa, nyris, 2026)
  Benchmarks CLIP-family, DINOv2/3, SigLIP2, Jina, Cohere embedders on
  industrial image-to-image instance retrieval; domain-tuned models
  beat general foundation models.
  Include? YES, framed narrowly as a retrieval-benchmark reference

* VLM4Rec (Valencia et al., USC course project)
  Uses LLaVA-NeXT to caption product images into text, then retrieves
  via Sentence-BERT - beats fusion architectures. Supports MULTIVIEW's

  Include? YES, with caveat (course project, not peer-reviewed)

* Zero Shot Composed Image Retrieval (Kakarla & Bulusu Venkata, GMU)
  Compares zero-shot CLIP vs. BLIP-2+Q-Former vs. failed Retrieval-DPO

  dual-encoding.
  Include? YES, supporting citation

* Evaluating UX in Conversational Recommender Systems
  (Mahmud et al., OZCHI '25)
  Systematic review of 23 UX studies on CRS; catalogs UEQ/ResQue/SUS

  Include? YES, for your evaluation-methodology chapter

* Fine-Grained ZS-CIR (CVSI)
  CLIP + BLIP-2 caption fusion for composed image retrieval (reference

  Include? MAYBE - related-but-distinct paradigm

* Scaling Prompt-Instructed ZS-CIR (InstructCIR)
  LLaVA-captioned synthetic triplets to train CIR without human

  Include? MAYBE - same reasoning

* Vision-Free Retrieval (LexiCLIP)
  Converts images to VLLM text descriptions, retrieves in single

  Include? MAYBE - useful for a limitations/discussion section on

* VLM2Rec (Kim et al., POSTECH)

4. NEW LITERATURE TO CONSIDER ADDING (fills genuine gaps)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Your review is strong on CLIP/VLM retrieval and LLM shopping agents but
has NO literature at all on voice+visual fusion, AR-UX evaluation
methodology, or DSR validity frameworks - despite MULTIVIEW building
all three. Recommended additions, ranked by fit:

Voice + multimodal search (your biggest literature gap):
1. MultiVox (Selvakumar et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.10859) - benchmark
   showing SOTA omni-modal voice assistants fail to jointly reason
   over speech + visual context. Directly motivates why MULTIVIEW's
   voice+CLIP/LLaVA fusion is a real, unsolved problem.
2. Cross-Modal Denoising for Speech-Image Retrieval (Zhou et al.,
   2024, arXiv:2408.13705) - closest technical precedent for
   speech-query -> product-image retrieval.
3. PSCon: Product Search Through Conversations (Zou et al., SIGIR '25,
   arXiv:2502.13881) - human-collected conversational product-search
   dataset with 6 subtasks; useful for framing your voice-query
   pipeline.

AR/3D and conversion:
4. UX Evaluation of AR: A Systematic Literature Review (Graser et al.,
   CENTRIC 2024) - finds only 3 standardized AR-UX questionnaires
   exist, justifying your custom Likert approach.
5. Virtual try-on and purchase intention (European Journal of
   Innovation Management, 2025) - empirical n=238 study; AR effect on
   purchase intention runs through perceived usefulness/value.
6. AR in e-commerce: dual value/barrier perspective on Gen Z (2025) -
   balances your AR coverage with adoption barriers, useful for
   limitations discussion.

Generative fallback imagery:
- No academic precedent found for Pollinations.ai/Stable-Diffusion-as-
  retrieval-fallback in retail. Worth stating explicitly in your
  thesis as a novelty claim rather than an oversight.
  

DSR methodology (strengthens your methodology chapter):
7. Artifact Validity in DSR (Kroop, DESRIST 2025, arXiv:2502.11199) -
   compares 3 DSR frameworks incl. Hevner's across 5 validity
   dimensions.
8. Validity in Design Science (Larsen et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.09466)
   - newer validity framework to triangulate against Hevner.
9. Holistic Evaluations in DSR (Springer LNBIP, 2025) - 6x10x8 matrix
   for matching artifact type to evaluation method; gives explicit
   justification for your CLIP-score + response-time + Likert combo.

Evaluation methodology:
10. CLIPScore (Hessel et al., EMNLP 2021) - the actual metric origin
    for "CLIP relevance score" evaluation; should be cited explicitly
    even though older.
11. UniECS (Liang et al., CIKM 2025, arXiv:2508.13843) - gated
    cross-modal fusion handling missing modalities + M-BEER
    benchmark; architecturally closer to your "unify everything" goal
    than VLM4Rec/VLM2Rec, deployed at Kuaishou (+2.74% CTR, +8.33%
    revenue).

Confirmed gap (no new papers found):
- No 2025-2026 papers reuse Pix3D for retrieval/AR (only the original
  2018 Sun et al. dataset paper exists) - reinforces that your use of
  Pix3D for unified retrieval+AR evaluation is genuinely novel.


5. BOTTOM LINE
----------------

- Of the 23 PDFs, 7 are strong, direct fits (Beyond Text, VL-CLIP,
  MOON/MOON2.0/MOON Embedding, Amazon multimodal retrieval, Turning
  Adversaries into Allies) - these should anchor your "prior art in
  multimodal e-commerce retrieval" cluster.
- ~8 are moderately relevant - good supporting/contextual citations,
  especially for LLaVA justification and evaluation methodology.
- ~8 are tangential (pure text-based LLM shopping agents/CRS) - keep
  these light-touch, since over-citing them dilutes your multimodal
  argument rather than strengthening it.
- Your literature review has zero coverage of voice-search research,
  AR-UX measurement standards, and DSR validity literature - all
  directly relevant to what you built. Adding 4-6 of the papers in
  Section 4 would close real gaps rather than pad the count.
